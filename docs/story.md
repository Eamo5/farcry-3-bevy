# Far Cry 3 story and missions

The story, missions and side activities are the game's own **Domino** graphs:
Lua generated by Ubisoft's visual scripting tool, run here unchanged on an
embedded Lua 5.1. Around them this project implements what the engine
provided: the box protocol, callbacks and timers, the mission manager, and the
natives the graphs call. Nothing from the game is stored in the repo; the
converter packs the scripts and the world data they need into the cache.

| crate | what |
|---|---|
| `fc3_domino` | the box runtime (`prelude.lua`): loading classes, `Boxes`, instances, engine calls |
| `fc3_story` | the mission system and the natives; runs headless or in the game |
| `fc3_game::story`, `story_actors`, `mission_layers` | the host in the game: what the scripts see, what they ask for |
| `fc3_tool story-run` | the story headless with a stand-in world (testing) |

## What the cache holds

`fc3convert` writes (see `fc3_formats::story` and `scripts`):

* `scripts/domino.pak`: every Domino script the game references.
* `story/entities.bin`: every entity the story may need, from every world
  sector and metasector, every mission layer included: those on mission
  layers, those with a script, trigger, zone, scene, marker or group, and
  those scripts name. Each keeps its components and its shape fields.
* `story/*.fcb`, `story/strings.bin`, `story/speechlength.bin`: the generated
  files the mission system reads as they are (the mission block table, map
  data, safehouses, spawn points, objective markers, templates with rewards,
  the localised strings and the spoken lines' lengths).
* `characters/all_kits.txt`: every graphic kit, by id, for the story's
  characters.
* `story/moves.txt`: the engine's named moves (`graphics\move\movemgrnamed.bin`,
  read by `fc3_formats::moves::parse_move_db`), each with its states and
  the clip each plays: a QTE move's `Intro`, `Struggle`, `Exit`, `Fail`; a
  contextual action's `STP_..._Wait`.
* `story/qte_moves.txt`: the QTE moves (`fc3_formats::qte`): each move's
  tree of states with the QTE results they play under and their clips, and
  each clip's QTE events (`fc3tool qte-moves [write | <move>...]` prints a
  move or writes the table).
* `story/ca_moves.txt`: the moves the story's contextual actions name, with
  their clips (from the move database; the few it lacks matched by name).
* `story/shop_items.txt`: the shop's weapons as scripts know them (model,
  library archetype, the string id of the shop name), from the entity
  library.
* `cinema/*.seq`: every cinematic sequence as a timeline, its facial tracks
  included, and its player-camera clips' head look (`HeadLook`: speed,
  return delay and speed, least and most pitch/roll/yaw). While a scene
  shows the player's own eyes (no camera shot), the mouse or stick turns
  his head within those limits (the opening's escape: 20 degrees up or
  down, 35 either side), easing back once left alone
  (`player::SceneHeadLook`); a scene holding the player without any view
  of its own leaves his view his to turn. `FC3_LOOK_DEBUG` logs what holds
  the view twice a second; `FC3_LOOK_TEST` feeds a steady turn (as a
  mouse would) for checking it. Without either, a mouse (or stick) turning
  for two seconds in play while the view stays still (no scene camera
  holding it) logs one warning naming what may be holding it (a scene
  hold, cutscene, takedown, the weapon wheel, a mounted gun, cursor capture
  or the pad), so an unexpected hold shows in an ordinary run's log.
* `facial/faces.pak`, `facial/definitions.txt`: every face rig and facial
  clip (named ones and spoken lines' lip-sync), and which rig a definition
  key names (`fc3_formats::facial`); `facial/kit_faces.txt`: the face
  definition each graphic kit's characters wear (from the entity library's
  archetypes).
* `video/*.bik`: the single-player Bink videos, kept as they are.

## The box protocol

A script sets the global `export` to a class table. Loading stores it in
`Boxes[GetPathID(path)]` (the archive's CRC-64 path hash, as a decimal
string), gives it `_meta`/`_type`, and runs `Create(cbox)` once to declare
dependencies (boxes, globals scripts, libraries, resources). Instances are
`setmetatable(t, class._meta)` with `Init(t, cbox)`; stateless boxes are used
as the class table itself. Every call from the engine goes through the class
(`inst._type[method]`). Entity ids cross as decimal strings of the 64-bit id.

## The mission system

* The root graph runs on an always-loaded entity; it registers every mission
  (`RegisterMission`), then starts the first chapter.
* A mission that starts running gets its main graph, on an entity of its own:
  scanned (registering its blocks), then entered.
* A block (checkpoint) that starts running activates its mission layers and
  the blocks it references (shared `*_common` content), and starts the script
  of its zone entity.
* Completion and failure come back to the boxes listening on the mission.
* Mission layers have four states (disabled, enabled, active, deactivated);
  the host shows the present ones.

## Natives

About 470 natives are reachable from single-player graphs. Those whose result
scripts ignore become `Command::Native` for the host (by name, with their
arguments); the queries are implemented in `fc3_story::natives` against the
story's state and the `View` the host sends each frame. Some engine behaviour
is the story's own:

* **Triggers**: proximity boxes and spheres (`vectorSize`, turned with the
  entity) and 2D regions (`hidShapePoints`, `fHeight`), tested against the
  bodies (a capsule, not a point) of the player and every character the
  host reports; the player's way since the last frame counts, so a thin
  trigger across a path isn't stepped over. Enabling a trigger (or listening
  to it again) reports whoever is already inside.
* **Dialogue**: one line at a time per speaker, by priority, queued or
  superseding as asked, timed from the speech length table; the host plays
  the voice and shows the subtitle.
* **Contextual actions**: the action each character is on, with its events
  raised on the action entity (`CA_Select`, `CA_Start`, `CA_End`,
  `CA_Interrupt`); the host moves the character and reports arrival.
* **Look-at triggers**: `EnterFOV` / `LeaveFOV` when the player is within
  the trigger's range and it is inside its share of the view's half angle.
* **HUD texts**: one per slot; a new one interrupts the shown one (when it
  is interruptible), waits for it (when it may queue) or is turned away, as
  `DisplayText`'s 0 / 2 / 1 tell the scripts.
* **Message and tutorial boxes**: the host shows them over a paused game;
  the answer goes back as `ACCEPT` or `CANCEL`. `PushTutorialBox` queues its
  localized title, body and footer too; a high-priority tutorial goes next
  without replacing the current box's answer target. Action placeholders
  such as `[Interact]` and `[ThrowRock]` use the current device's bindings.
  Holding the close control does not dismiss the next queued tutorial.
* **Teleports**: a teleported player is followed by `DominoGameReady`, which
  boxes behind a loading screen wait for.
* **Safehouse travel**: full-map destinations join `CSafeHouseComponent` to
  `entMarkerEntity` and the marker's `entSpawnPointEntity`. Arrival uses that
  spawn point's position and heading, not the outpost centre. Authored initial
  locks, saved unlocks, loaded layers, the global travel modifier and active
  mission/block permissions gate the transaction. An active block overrides
  its main mission's default; unrelated missions keep their own locks. Amanaki's
  tutorial selection reports `jas_080_fast_travel` only after travel succeeds.
* **Trigger contacts**: authored player-only regions and the proximity
  trigger's non-player flag filter both host overlaps and live character
  positions. NPCs moving or streaming out cannot fail a player-only mission
  boundary; the player leaving it still delivers `Leave`.
* **Specific gameplays** (`CreateSpecificGameplay`: QTEs, minigames, boss
  fights): the host plays them; they end (`OnDestroyed`) when destroyed, when
  the host says, or (one the host doesn't run) when a sequence sets a QTE's
  result (`QteSetResult`); a QTE gameplay hears the result instead.
* **Reinforcements**: a point's mercs (by archetype) come in under ids of
  their own and join its groups; the box hears who came (`OnSpawned`).
* **Saves**: blocks a save left running play again as their missions
  register.
* **Script control**, **on-screen timers**, **water levels**: state and
  timing in the story. Visible named timers expose their localized labels
  and current values to the host HUD; hidden timers still run and deliver
  callbacks. Water-level commands still need host integration.

## In the game

* A new game plays the story (the options' "New game: story", or
  `FC3_STORY=0/1`); a continued game plays it when `story.txt` sits next to
  the save. Checkpoints write it; dying in a mission, or a failure that asks
  for it, goes back to the last one.
* **Characters**: pawns on present layers spawn near the player dressed from
  their kit. Armed ones whose army is hostile to the player's fight with the
  enemy AI. While the story drives one (its AI starts under script control,
  or it is on a contextual action) it keeps seeing and hearing; when it
  notices something (a thrown rock, the player, a body) its action is broken
  off (`CA_Interrupt`) and its AI takes over. Its AI state goes back to the
  scripts' state listeners. At an action's spot, the action's looping clip
  stands in for the idle.
* **Objective markers** show on screen, at its edge when off it, on the
  minimap's rim and on the map; the GPS leads to the objective when the
  player has set no waypoint.
* **Music** (`music`): the music service's states from the object templates:
  menu and pause tracks, combat music while enemies fight the player, an
  exploration piece now and then in calm, and the states missions start and
  stop. Priority decides; during a mission only states at or above the
  service's mission priority play.
* **Rewards** go to the player's XP and money. The scripts' own handouts
  arrive as natives the host applies: `GivePlayerXP` and the outposts'
  `GivePlayerOutpostXPBonus`, `AddPlayerMoney` / `WithdrawPlayerMoney` /
  `SetPlayerMoney`, `AddArmor`, `RemoveLootItem`, `GiveAmmoToPlayer`
  (magazines for every gun carried), `RefillGadgetAmmo` and
  `EnableWholeTattoo` (saved). `AddBonusPlan` / `RemoveBonusPlan` lend an
  ability for a while by its bonus plan's name (the last mission's
  takedowns), saved with the progress.
* **Skill tree**: the converter writes `progression/skills.txt` from the
  `AbilityService` template (`fc3tool skills [--bonuses]` prints it). The
  journal's skills page lays each branch out in its authored rows and
  columns. An ability opens when its mission completes
  (`UnlockOnMissionComplete`, recorded as missions finish and synced from the
  story's state on start) or when the scripts call
  `CAPI_Player.SetAbilityUpgradable`; free roam opens every one. It then needs
  its prerequisites, its statistic (takedowns by kind, handgun, melee and
  grenade kills, money spent at stores, attachments bought, undetected
  outposts, medicine crafted, bull sharks, relics) and its points. What an
  ability does is mapped by id in `Perks::from_progress`, with its strength
  from its bonus plans: health slots, reload speed, spread, swimming, fall,
  explosion, fire and melee resistance, medkit and manual heals, regeneration
  delay, syringe duration, loot doubling and sale price, and the takedown
  family (no takedowns at all before the Takedown ability). Levels follow the
  game's XP table. Mechanics behind abilities (by id): sliding needs
  `SP_Slide`, a held grenade cooks with `SP_CookThrowables` (too long and it
  goes off in hand), sprinting breaks off a reload until `SP_SprintReload`,
  swapping and breath scale with their bonuses, the scope-breath abilities
  hold a scope's breath longer (`SP_SniperHoldBreathA`/`B`),
  `SP_FastAimedReload` keeps a pistol or sniper rifle in the sights,
  `SP_ShootOnZipline` lets pistols fire from a zip line (nothing else does),
  `SP_MountedGunHandling` slows a mounted gun's heat, the bow abilities draw
  faster and steadier, `SP_CrouchRun` hurries a crouch with the sprint key,
  `SP_LongerSprintBurst` adds its sprint speed, `SP_VehicleExtraHealth`
  softens hits on the vehicle driven, `SP_ArmorDefense` stretches body
  armour, and `SP_SilentRun` quietens
  steps (guards hear steps out of sight, a sprint farthest and a crouch
  hardly at all), and `SP_LootTakedown` searches a takedown's victim as he
  falls. The tattoo grows with what is learned (below). Not done yet: the
  knife throw's extra XP, and the statistics of activities that aren't in
  the game (supply drops, Wanted Dead).
* **XP**: the converter adds the game's XP rewards (`XPRewards`) to
  `progression/skills.txt`, each looting prototype's own XP (`XpGiven`) to
  its source, and collectibles' XP to their items. A kill is worth the
  victim's XP (by the kit he wears, or the animal's own) times how he died:
  headshots, explosions, stealth kills and each kind of takedown (the
  takedown code notes which killed whom). Radio towers and collectibles
  give the game's amounts.
* **Weapons' handling**: the converter writes `weapons/stats.txt` from the
  entity library's `WeaponProperties:FC3/*` prototypes (`CWeaponProperties`:
  damage a bullet, fire rate, magazine, reload, draw and holster times,
  pellets, silence), each joined to its `weapons:FC3/*` model. A swap takes
  the one weapon's holster time and the other's draw time.
  `weapons::weapon_defs` puts them on the weapons whose model they name
  (the launchers, bow and flamethrower keep their own blast and burn
  damage; mounted guns their belts), so every copy of the table (loadout,
  saves, pickups) carries them; without the table the built-in values stay.
* **Shop and loot**: the converter adds the shopping database's supplies and
  loot to `progression/recipes.txt`: each item's price, rounds per purchase,
  shop placement and (for body armour) its pickup's armour value, and the
  loot groups (cash, items, ammunition and gadgets, each with its chance and
  range, resolved to the shop items that sell them) with each looting
  prototype's groups and graphic kit. The supplies tab sells the database's
  consumables and ammunition (for the guns carried) at its prices; refilling
  everything costs the boxes it takes. Body armour absorbs bullets, blasts
  and blows until spent and shows beside health. A searched body rolls its
  kit's own loot groups: cash, loot items into the rucksack (as far as it
  holds), ammunition and gadgets; kits the game gives no loot keep the older
  cash and ammo. Loot chests roll their prototype's own groups the same way,
  and armour pickups put on the shop armour's value. The SELL tab sells loot
  by the stack or all at once, at the item's price scaled by the sale-price
  skill.
* **Crafting** follows the converted recipes throughout. Each gear chain
  (holster, rucksack, syringe kit, wallet, ammo, munitions, fuel and rocket
  packs, grenade pouch, quiver) crafts tier by tier, and capacities are its
  crafted tiers' bonuses on the base kit: weapon slots, syringes, wallet,
  grenades, explosives, arrows, loot, and the ammo pouch's tier. Free roam
  keeps its own kit and gains what is crafted beyond it. Syringes are the
  potion recipes, kept by recipe (`Progress::potions`; older saves' syringes
  map onto the recipes that do the same) and lasting as their recipe says.
  The ones the game plays: wildlife, enemy and plant vision, fireproof, deep
  dive, sprint burst, animal resistance, animal repellent, steady aim,
  one-hit kills and invincibility, and the temporary health boost
  (`Temphealthboost`): its recipe's
  extra health slots (read from its effect) on top and a full heal; those
  bars show in amber and are lost as they're hurt, never regenerating. The
  HUD shows as many bars as the player's health has. Special arrows are the arrow-tip recipes: each tips one arrow with a
  grenade or a molotov from the inventory (the shop no longer sells them).
  The journal shows the rucksack's fill, loot to sell included.
* **The tattoo** (`tattoo`): the first-person arms are Jason's own model,
  and his left arm's material multiplies in the tattoo's design (second UV
  set) where the mask's piece id is revealed. The skill tree's
  `TattooParts` give each part's ids. A skill part shows one id per learned
  ability of the part; the story's pieces (`Dennis1`, `Citra1`, the bosses',
  `Final`) show once the scripts open them with `SetAbilityUpgradable`, or
  all at once in free roam; the relics part shows one dot per relic.
  `CAPI_Player.ShowTattoo` hides or shows it all. The converter adds the
  parts to `progression/skills.txt` and brings the design and mask into
  `textures/` (the mask uncompressed, so its ids stay exact). The design
  wraps (the arm's second UVs run past the 0..1 square), and tattooed parts
  never go on the translated original shaders, whose skin shader would draw
  the whole design. The right arm's material names the downloadable tribal
  tattoo in the same slot; it keeps the standard material, the skin without
  it.
* **Handbook**: the converter writes `progression/handbook.txt` from
  `wikiitems.lib` and the encyclopedia's categories (localised names and
  texts; the manual's pages left out, tutorials put under the tutorial
  subcategory their name gives). The journal's HANDBOOK tab lists each
  category's subcategories, a page of their entries and the one read. In
  the story a page opens from the start, with its mission, or with its
  script group (`UnlockItemsFromScript`, which also opens the shop's
  script-locked supplies; groups are saved). Pages the engine opens as the
  player meets what they describe: an animal's once one is killed or
  skinned, a plant's once gathered (by name, saved as met), a weapon's once
  held (or a gadget carried), a vehicle's once boarded, the parachute's and
  wingsuit's once owned (the handbook's own names for them, matched on
  letters and digits; a few differ, mapped in `weapon_page`/`vehicle_page`),
  and as many of a kind of collectible as have been found. Free roam opens
  all of them.
* **UI type**: Windows' Bahnschrift replaces Bevy's built-in monospace
  (closer to the game's own bitmap Trade Gothic, and it has the curly quotes
  and accents the game's text uses). `FC3_UI_FONT=<file>` picks another, and
  `FC3_UI_FONT=builtin` keeps Bevy's.
* **Contextual actions**: a character walks to the spot along the navmesh,
  plays the action's start clip, then its loop in place of the idle; a
  patrol-path action walks the path its parameters name and ends at its end.
* **Quick-time events** (`qte`, the `QTEGSF` gameplay) run as the game's
  move data says (`fc3_formats::qte`). A QTE move is a tree of states, each
  playing under QTE results (`QTEn_Result`, n the QTE's id plus one: 3 none,
  0 in progress, 1 failed, 2 succeeded); the first in order whose conditions
  (and its groups') hold plays, a group with no child to play its own clip.
  The QTEs are events in the clips (`QuickTimeEvent*`): a press, a hold or a
  mash asks for an input (`selInputs`, the engine's own names: `Button_1`,
  `RT`, `LT`, `LeftStickLeft`...; the controls answer them, e.g. the triggers
  are fire and aim), with the prompt (the game's word for the answer) and a
  meter for a mash or a hold; a "zombie" QTE waits for a script's
  `QteSetResult`;
  others end or reset a QTE, end the sequence, or message the scripts
  (`PlayerDie`). A state's clip plays to its end before the next is chosen,
  unless a QTE is decided and the state no longer holds (an answered press
  crawls at once). A state with an anchor (`cnAA`) plays from the scene's
  anchor; others go on from where the last clip left the player (the escape
  crawl moves him along). The scripts hear `QTE<id>_InProgress`, then
  `QTE<id>_Success` or `QTE<id>_Fail`, and as it ends `QTE_Success` (or
  `QTE_Failure`, a QTE left failed or the move having killed the player)
  and `ExoticQteGSFEnded`. A gameplay of another name with a
  `QTE_move_file` is one too (JAS_150's escape from the ropes,
  `JAS_160_EscapeRopes`, waits on `QTE_Success`): the autopilot no longer
  ends it as a gameplay the game doesn't play, nor a sequence's
  `QteSetResult` as one the host doesn't run.
  Each QTE also has a progress, 0 to 1 (a mash's intensity, a hold's time
  held, or what a clip's `QuickTimeEventSetPMSValues` sets), which the
  moves read as `Interactive_Float<n>` (n the QTE's id plus one): a state
  may play only within a progress range (`FIcM`), 40 of the 122 QTE moves.
  A push's pose follows its mash; JAS_160's mass grave climbs `Reach1` to
  `Reach5` as each reach's clip sets QTE 2's progress on, then the way out
  by a mash. A posed state (`pMCT`) plays a moment of a longer clip from a
  time (the climb's poses are times in one clip), chosen again as the
  progress moves on.
  BAM_055's tomb ends this way: the crawl (QTE 0 and 1, the triggers in
  turn) runs while the rock rolls; the script hears the zombie QTE 2 start,
  waits for the player to crawl near the way out, and decides it (the
  `Exit` clip) before the rock's "DeathHeight" point, or fails it (`Dead`).
* **Shops**: liberated outposts and the world's shopkeepers keep one; a
  weapon bought raises `CPawnBuyItemEvent` (archetype, `Name`, the shop name's
  string id) on the player. The converter joins the shopping database to the
  entity library in `story/shop_weapons.txt`; supported base weapons use their
  authored prices. Shared-model signature/DLC variants are not treated as
  base weapons. `OverrideShopMenu` restricts the first-shop tutorial to its
  requested item, until the script releases it. Purchases made while the shop
  pauses gameplay are retained and delivered once when play resumes.
  Base weapon freebies follow the catalog's `TowerN` thresholds, not a
  one-weapon-per-tower list. The first tower frees the 1911, 6P9 and STG-90;
  the reward notification lists every supported weapon freed by that tower.
  Free stock still has to be collected from a shop; it is not granted on unlock.
* **Ingredient loot**: skinning and plant gathering raise `CPawnLootItemEvent`
  (item key, `Name`, localized item id) after the live inventory snapshot updates.
  Events survive paused tutorial/menu frames and are delivered once. The
  converted `progression/recipes.txt` table joins authored loot groups to
  shopping items and source prototypes; shared-model pigs/rare animals are
  not used as base ingredient identities. `GetLootQuantity` reads carried
  skins and leaves by localized id, combining plants yielding the same leaf;
  consumption and save loading change those queries without inventing events.
  Scripts' plant/animal looting permissions gate interactions, which consume
  their input press so another interaction cannot reuse it that frame.
   All eighteen authored plant families are recognized; unknown names no longer
   become Arrowroot. Existing saves retain the first eight species' slot indices.
   Leaves stay carried until explicitly consumed, rather than auto-making medkits.
   Mission-layer collectibles now use the same interaction path as world pickups,
   excluding colocated minimap markers and already-collected ids on restreaming.
  The host also shows each authored dynamic checklist row, formatting its
   localized collection counter and independently updating or hiding it.
* **Authored crafting**: the journal offers Medicine and Simple Rucksack using
  the installed recipe's ingredients, completed-mission unlock and event identity.
  Medicine consumes one green leaf from any matching species; the rucksack
  consumes two boar hides and applies the authored +16 loot-capacity modifier.
  Capacity and unlock checks happen before any ingredient changes. Crafted gear
  persists in saves; crafting events survive journal frames and are delivered once
  as `DominoEvent_CraftRecipe(kind, sName)`. The script-owned crafting tutorial
  requires two committed Medicine crafts followed by the Simple Rucksack, and
  rejects early exit, other recipes and the skills page. Its two localized stage
  titles come from the original UI. Saves retain partial tutorial progress and
  completion so reopening B40 cannot require ingredients already consumed.
  Other gear and syringe recipes still use
  legacy rules; this is not the original menu's visual/layout reproduction.
* **Pause-menu callbacks**: opening the menu, world map, crafting and skills
  pages, and exiting the pause menu raise their authored generic events once.
  Retained purchase/loot/craft events follow refreshed inventory queries. Menus
  pump the story at zero elapsed time: UI callbacks work without running mission
  timers, gameplay input or autopilot repositioning. The PlayerParamsService
  template's `JAS_080_main` gates lock crafting/skills until the tutorial overrides
  them or the mission completes. Explicit later menu locks remain independent.
* **Story wildlife**: supported normal animals retain their authored graphic
  kit and mission id while using the wildlife species' clips, behavior, health
  and death/skinning path. Their feet retain the story host's placement offset.
  Contextual actions and cinematics own their movement until released, and
  story-layer streaming owns their lifetime rather than generic carcass rot.
  Shared-model pigs and rare loot variants are not substituted as base animals;
  those unsupported families/variants still require their own implementations.
* **Player queries**: money, medkits, carry slots, equipped and carried
  weapons (using the shop's archetype names), gadgets, learned skills,
  stance, zip-line state, look angles, tagged story actors, difficulty and
  time of day come from live game state. Inventory ids returned to scripts
  resolve back to their archetypes when commands remove weapons or gadgets.
  Inventory spawn/remove callbacks follow host-reported availability;
  subscriptions made after an entity arrives still receive its spawn callback.
  Removing the rock or camera also disables its player control; gadget-wide
  removal clears carried explosives as well.
  Mission inventory backups restore those gadgets, carried ammunition and
  explosives, and the previously selected weapon, once.
  The camera accepts both the tablet and the binoculars granted by Dennis.
  New campaigns do not inherit free-roam starter medkits. Scripted medpack
  giving/removal respects the live pouch capacity and saturates at zero; these
  adjustments are not crafting events. The remaining sandbox medical/skill
  baseline still needs replacement by authored attributes.
* **Scene lifetime**: sequences retain their meta-sequence owner; stopping
  that owner releases its looping scenes and character overrides, without
  stopping unrelated scenes or the host's QTE phases.
* **Videos** (`video`, decoded by `fc3_bink`): full screen when the screen is
  the player (or names no material); else on the screen entity itself: the
  converter indexes the materials scripts name for screens (`BinkPlayer`'s
  `MaterialName`, found by the material files' own names) in
  `video/screens.txt`, and the parts of the placed entity with that material
  show the video self-lit (off the original shaders while it plays), getting
  their own material back when it ends; a picture in a corner stands in until
  the screen is found. The stereo bed and the voice track (`FC3_VIDEO_VOICE`,
  by default 1) mixed; skippable; the story hears them start and end (videos
  also play without a story). `FC3_SCREEN_TEST=<video>` plays one on the
  nearest placed screen within 40 m.
* **Phone calls** show the caller in a panel until the scripts' `PhoneCallEnd`.
* **Story teleports** keep to the height they give (a cave floor, not the rock
  over it). Actor and prop destinations are resolved when the native runs,
  including chained teleports. Actors not streamed in yet spawn at their new
  positions; already loaded actors replan their contextual-action routes.
  Scripted collision enable/disable state also applies to later-streamed roots.
* **Mission interactions**: holding the bound interact control uses a nearby
  enabled, usable proximity trigger. Prompts use localized interaction text
  when supplied; use, look and angle restrictions are checked. Releasing the
  control or changing targets cancels the hold, and one hold triggers at most
  one use. A gamepad's shared reload control is suppressed during the hold.
* **Faces** (`faces`): characters with a `CFacialPoseComponent` get its face
  rig; a scene's facial track poses the face over the body clip, and a spoken
  line plays its lip-sync clip on the speaker. Generic characters (enemies,
  villagers) wear the face their graphic kit's archetype names (a kit without
  one borrows a same-faction kit's). Every face blinks on the library's idle
  loop, each from its own phase, outside scenes.
* **Barks** (`barks`): the converter joins each voice's stimulus resources

* **Vehicle handling** (`vehicles::tuning`, `fc3_formats::handling`): the
  converter reads each vehicle archetype's `CVehicleWheeledPhysComponent.
  WheeledParams`, `BoatParams` or `ParagliderParams` and its driver's
  camera `FOV`, by
  descriptor folder, into `vehicles/handling.txt`. The units are the ones
  the engine's own field notes (in `FC3.dll`) give: engine power in
  horsepower; each gear's shift range in km/h; `fGearBoxTopSpeed`, the
  speed the gearing reaches at the top RPM, and the engine cut-off speeds
  in m/s; the camera's speeds in km/h. A car's pull is the engine's torque
  (its horsepower at the optimal revs, times the torque factor at the top)
  through the gear the gearbox holds at that speed and the final drive the
  gearbox's top speed sets: the 4x4 pulls about 12 kN in first, 7.7 in
  second and 5.2 in third, and its engine cuts off above 20 m/s (72 km/h;
  16 m/s in reverse), as in the original. Air drag is the data's
  coefficient and frontal area; the steering lock runs from the data's lock
  at rest to its lock at top speed; the tyres' grip is the sideways
  friction limit, wheel by wheel. Each wheel's spring and dampers are the
  data's Havok figures, which are per kilogram of the chassis (the notes'
  N/m and N/(m/s) are Havok's own doc strings; read as plain newtons the
  4x4's 29 N/m spring could not hold it up): the spring pushes strength x
  mass per metre pressed in, the dampers compression or relaxation x mass
  per m/s, and the spring's unloaded reach is set so a quarter of the car
  rests on each wheel at the model's own ride height. Four of the data's
  wheels stand for ours (front pair and last pair; a six-wheeler's dummy
  middle axle has no grip). Only `bDriving` wheels pull, and the player's
  handbrake (Space) locks only the `bHandBrake` wheels, which then keep
  `WheelLockFrictionScale` of their grip; AI drivers' and wrecks' brakes
  hold every wheel. The chassis takes its angular damping, its extra
  gravity (`fAeroExtraGravity`), its lift coefficient (negative: downforce,
  with the frontal area, as drag is), gravity's factor with no wheel down
  (`WheelAirborneGravityModifier`, eased in over its time, above its
  speed) and the stabiliser's turn rate in the air (`fStabilizerAccel`: 0
  for most cars, which then land however they come down; ours rights
  them at 4). A boat's motor pushes at its thrust (an acceleration per
  unit of mass) times its revs, which come up at the motor's rate; the
  water damps it ahead and to the side by its friction figures as the
  engine does it (FC3.dll's boat update, with the params at 0x330 in its
  physics object): for each of its buoyancy spheres in the water (those of
  any size; the zodiac, jet ski and patrol boat float on four), it adds
  minus the friction (`fForwardFriction` or `fReverseFriction` by the way it goes, side) times
  the speed along that axis, and multiplies the sum by the mass, so the
  frictions are rates per sphere, linear in the speed as
  `bSquareVelocityForLinearFriction` is 0 for all. Ours scales the sum by
  the share of the hull's corners in the water (`handling::Boat::spheres`),
  which tops the zodiac out at 30 m/s, the jet ski at 28 and the patrol boat
  at 20. It floats as the engine floats it, on the same spheres: each at
  its offset from the engine's centre of mass (`vectorCenterOfMassOffset`,
  ours too; `fSpheresZOffset` and `fVolumeScale` are, as their notes say,
  not used with listed spheres), dropping by `fSpheresZOffsetMax` per
  `fSpheresZOffsetMaxSpeed` of speed (unclamped, as the engine has it: the
  hull rises as it gets going, 0.85 m for the zodiac at 22 m/s); each
  one's submerged cap (pi h^2 (3r - h) / 3) is summed, and the water's
  weight that much less the boat's mass (1000 kg/m^3 times the volume less
  the mass, in newtons, as FC3.dll's boat update applies it through
  Havok's `applyForce`) pushes up at the centre of buoyancy. Gravity takes
  its `GravityModifier` with any sphere in the water, else
  `GravityModifierAirborne`; at each sphere in the water, `fUpDownFriction`
  damps its rising and falling and `fDownFriction` (with the speed squared,
  as deep as it is in) its going down into the water, and
  `fUpRotateFriction` its yaw; past `fStabilizerRollAngleActivation` the
  stabiliser rights it at `StabilizerAccel`. So the zodiac rests with the
  water 0.3 m under its origin (its tubes at the waterline), the jet ski on
  the surface and the patrol boat as deep as its hull. A boat without
  listed spheres still floats on its hull's corners. The drive test logs a
  boat's bounds.
  The rudder
  swings from its angle at rest to its angle at its top speed. Throttling
  against the way it goes, the motor pushes at `fEngineBrakingPower`
  instead; a boat with a `Brake` (the patrol boat's 5 m/s²) brakes on Space;
  the hull's angular damping is its `fAngularDamping`. A hang
  glider flies by its `ParagliderParams` (`vehicles::Glide`), as their
  field notes describe them: the bank and pitch each ease towards the
  rate the angle still to go asks for (times the roll or pitch velocity
  scale, up to its maximum, reached at its acceleration); the bank turns it
  at the roll-to-turn figure, in full once it has the turn speed; pitch
  changes its speed (`fPitchSpeedScale` per radian, nose down faster, up to
  the maximum speed), and climbing with the speed faded the nose drops
  back; the brake slows it towards its floor at the braking rate, and with
  the stick left alone it returns to its base speed at the restore rate. It
  always sinks at the down speed (the low-altitude one under its glide
  height, the malfunction one below its health threshold), and under the
  landing altitude its landing figures (base and top speed, bank, pitch,
  sink) ease in over the transition time. The game's take-off run follows
  authored displacement curves; ours runs straight out at the launch's
  height for the take-off time, its collision off (gliders stand on the
  slope they launch down), and the wing's collision is a thin slab over a
  narrow frame. Down on the ground or the water, or held back half a second,
  it has landed until it falls off an edge again. The driving camera
  widens with speed by the vehicle's own angles, as a share of the
  player's field of view (`vehicles::SpeedFov`), and in first person the
  driver's look is held within `CFCXVehicle`'s `vehicleMinLookAngle` and
  `vehicleMaxLookAngle` (x is pitch and z yaw; the data counts pitch down
  as positive: open vehicles allow -75 to -80, closed cars -10, the glider
  and wingsuit +90 and more, and we take yaw the same way round; the only
  lopsided yaw ranges are the APC's and passengers').

  each kind of sound (`npc::NoiseKind`: interesting,

### Lens drops

On coming up out of the water, the lens now shows the player's own droplet
effect. The player prototype's `ScreenDropletParticle` names it in
`psScreenDroplet` (`FC3_Gameplay_Water.Player.ScreenDropplets_Emerge`); its
other values (count, duration, life, speed, size) look like defaults, as
other pawns carry them with no effect named. The converter caches the
effect (`particles/screen-droplets.xml`) with its drop atlas, normal map and
reflection map (`fc3_gfx_waterdripplet_8x2_test` and `_n`,
`fc3_gfx_environmentmap_02`).

The effect is one emitter, one burst of 18 drops. They live 0.3 to 1.2 s,
are 0.5 to 1.25 cm across, and are fully there for half their life before
fading out. They start in a volume 0.2 m in front of the eye: 0.28 x 0.17 m
once its `RotOffset` turns it, which is the view's own size at that
distance. Their gravity (0.35, rising over their life) works in the eye's
own axes, as the sky's rain splash shows (its two halves pull out to the
sides and down). For these drops it points back at the eye, so they come
closer, growing and spreading out as they fade. The emitter's `StaticSize`
(17) can't be a scale: 17 times a drop's size is wider than the view.

`fc3_game::lens_drops` works the drops out from the emitter each frame and
draws them in a pass over the finished frame. The pass bends the view
through each drop's normal map (`DistortionStrength`, in screen
fractions), blurs it a little (`DistortionBlurStrength`), and lets the rims
catch the reflection map (`EnvMapStrength`, by how far the surface turns
away). The drops' own colour (`DiffuseMapStrength`, 4%) barely shows. The
flat overlay of `fc3_gfx_waterdrops_01` is gone. `FC3_LENS_DROPS_TEST=<s>`
sets the drops off that long into play, and `FC3_LENS_DROPS_CAPTURE=<png>`
saves a frame a fifth of a second later. Tests cover the drops spreading,
growing, fading and going, and the turned volume spanning the view.

Rain doesn't wet the lens in this game. Its rain effect
(`fc3_environment.weather_fx.fc3_rain`) is all in the world: drips and
droplets falling through volumes metres across, 4 to 9 m over the camera.
The other screen-droplet effects belong elsewhere. The sky library's
`ScreenDropletsSplash`, a six-emitter version of the surfacing drops with
splashes, is named by the skydiving gameplay (`CSkyDiving`'s
`m_dropletParticlesEffect`). `SWaterDropsData` sits with the vehicles'
data, for spray thrown at the view.

### Sharpshooter challenges (shooting galleries)

A country's activities include a sharpshooter challenge: a keeper
(`shooting_gallery.main.lua`, its `PawnInteractionListener`) who, used,
starts the gallery's gameplay (`CShootingGallerySniper`, `...Pistol`...:
`CreateSpecificGameplay` with the challenge's block and layers, its
difficulties' layers and the messages it is to broadcast), then hears how
it went (`shooting_gallery_mini_game_finished`, `_failed`, `_quit`,
`_cancelled`) and stops it. Nothing played it, so it ended a second in.
It is played now (`shooting_gallery`), from the gallery's settings and the
galleries' resources (`gsf::ShootingGallerySettings`,
`ShootingGalleryResources`):

* **The difficulty box.** Practice (free) or one of the gallery's three
  difficulties, each with its wager and kill target; a row the player
  can't afford isn't taken. Escape backs out (the cancel message).
* **The challenge's layers.** The base layer and the difficulty's come in
  (`Story::set_layer_state`); their spots register themselves with the
  player (`common/gsfspawnpointregister`: `SendGSFGenericEventToEntity <player>
  SpawnPointRegistration <kind> <spot>`), the shooter's spot and the birds'
  boxes (`BirdBox`). The player is placed at the shooter's spot facing the
  boxes, held there within the gallery's view limits, and lent its weapon
  (the resources' archetype, found by its family in the shop's table:
  `weapons:FC3/R700` is the 700), its magazine kept full.
* **Get ready, the countdown** (`ShootingMinigame_StartCountdown` sent, as
  the script waits for it), **then the waves**: each wave's birds (the
  gallery's animal, its model and fly and death clips) leave the loaded
  boxes one by one (or as a flock) after the wave's delay, fly off toward
  points within the gallery's frustum at their difficulty's speed, turning
  now and then by its angles, and fall when shot. A wave's time up, what's
  left of it gets away and the next comes.
* **The result.** The kill target reached, the wager comes back times the
  gallery's winnings; the last wave over without it, it's lost. Enter
  plays again (the difficulty box), Escape leaves: the finish or fail
  message goes to the keeper.
* **The autopilot** leaves the player at a gallery (as at the sniping
  challenge); with `FC3_GALLERY_TEST=<row>` that row is chosen and the
  birds are shot through the sights, and `FC3_GALLERY_SHOTS=<dir>`
  captures each box and the first wave.
* **Seen.** From a kept checkpoint with the player placed outside C3's
  outpost (`--pos` over a checkpoint now holds for its game snapshot too)
  and `--outpost-test`: the outpost taken, the activities in, the race
  keeper used (the race isn't played yet), then the sniper gallery's
  keeper: novice chosen ($100), the 700 lent, four boxes, ten birds shot,
  $200 won and the keeper told.

### Poker tables

Poker is Texas hold'em (`holdem`: the deck, the best five of seven cards,
the blinds, four streets, all-ins and side pots, the computer players'
choices by their chances against what calling costs) at a table the
gameplay's settings name (`gsf::PokerSettings`: its computer players,
their seats and ways of playing, the four buy-ins), played with the
table's own movie (`ACTI_Poker.feu`, `docs/ui.md`).

* **The bars' tables.** Each bar's table is an encounter prefab in its
  base block's main layer (`c3_poker`'s `C3_Poker_main`: the table, its
  chairs and anchors, the bartender and the bar's people), with a
  start-on-load script (`fc3_activities/poker/<cell>_poker`) that starts
  the table's gameplay when the player comes within 200 m and its three
  players are in, and stops it past 250 m. The country's activities master
  opens the block with its marker layer only, and nothing in the data
  brings the main layer in. The engine had it; here a base block's main
  layer that no script and no gameplay names comes in with any other of
  its block's layers (`fc3_story::standing_layers`; `fc3tool story-blocks
  standing` lists them).
* **Sitting down.** The computer players find the table (the centre
  anchor nearest them) and take the seats they ask for; the player has
  the one left. Near the free chair the prompt sits the player there,
  facing the table, the weapon away and the HUD down; the difficulty box
  takes a buy-in (practice is free), paid and played for as chips by
  everyone at the table. Leaving pays out the player's chips; going bust
  offers another game; cleaning the table wins every buy-in. The data has
  no blinds or stacks: practice starts everyone with 1000 chips, a buy-in
  with that many; the blinds are a fortieth and a twentieth of it,
  doubling every ten hands.
* **Keys.** Up and down set the bet by the big blind, Space or Enter
  checks, calls or raises to it, E folds, Q goes all in, Escape leaves,
  1 shows the table's totals. The boxes take Enter and Escape, and the
  mouse over their buttons.
* **The story's tables** (JAS_170's, Hoyt's) start on their script's
  event and play for nothing, a scene between hands as their scripts wait
  for them; under the autopilot (and `FC3_POKER_TEST=<row>`, which also
  sits at a bar's table and leaves after `FC3_POKER_HANDS`) the player's
  hands play themselves. SQC_100's is a bar's game: its script hears the
  player clean the table (`JasonWinsGame`) and leave.
* **Seen.** C3's bar (a kept checkpoint with C3's outpost taken, the
  player placed in the bar): the table's gameplay started, the player sat
  in the fourth seat, paid $100 and played six hands (blinds, calls,
  raises, folds, showdowns) to leave with $180.
* **Not yet.** The cards and chips on the table, the players seated and
  playing their moves, their voices, the help screen's odds, the
  scripted decks of Hoyt's table.
